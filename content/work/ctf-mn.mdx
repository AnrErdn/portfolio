## THE PROBLEM

CTF.mn had an identity problem. The platform ran Mongolia's premier competitive hacking competition, but looked like it was built in 2012. Cluttered navigation, no visual hierarchy, and zero brand personality. Participants knew the event was serious — the site didn't show it.

## THE SOLUTION

A full brand and UI rebuild from the ground up. New logo, new color system, new type hierarchy. Every page was redesigned to reflect the precision and intensity of CTF competition. The design had to feel technical without feeling cold.

## Process

**01 — Research and audit.** I mapped every page of the old site, catalogued what was broken, and interviewed two past participants about what frustrated them. The recurring complaint: "It looks untrustworthy." That defined the brief — credibility first, aesthetics second.

**02 — Brand direction.** I tested three visual directions: military-technical, brutalist dark, and clean minimal. The client chose a refined version of military-technical — high contrast, precise, with lime accent instead of the cliché red. The logo came from reducing the CTF monogram to its sharpest possible form.

**03 — UI execution.** I rebuilt the challenge browser, leaderboard, and landing page. Chose a dark base to reduce eye strain during long competition sessions — a direct ask from participants. Typography was Space Grotesk throughout for readability at all sizes.

## Outcome

The redesigned site launched before CTF 2025. The visual overhaul matched what the competition already was: Mongolia's most technically demanding CTF event.

## What I'd do differently

I'd run usability tests with participants earlier in the process. I made assumptions about the challenge browser that I later had to revise — user testing at the wireframe stage would have saved two rounds of revision.
